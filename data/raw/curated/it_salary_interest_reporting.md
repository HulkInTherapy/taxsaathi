## [IT-INT-OTHER-SOURCES] Bank and FD interest is taxable as "Income from other sources" and must be reported
section: 56
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: interest_income
verified: partial

Interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits (FDs), recurring deposits and post-office deposits is taxable under the head "Income from other sources" (section 56 of the Income-tax Act, 1961) and is added to your total income at your slab rate, in both the old and the new regime. It must be reported in your income-tax return even if the bank has already deducted TDS, and even if no TDS was deducted at all. FD interest is taxable year by year as it accrues, not only when the FD matures. TDS on interest is only a prepayment: if your slab rate is above 10%, you owe the difference when you file. In the old regime you can then claim section 80TTA (up to ₹10,000 of savings-account interest, below 60) or section 80TTB (up to ₹50,000 of all deposit interest, senior citizens); neither is available in the new regime. Salaried people can report interest in ITR-1 if they are otherwise eligible.

## [IT-INT-TDS-194A] TDS on interest by banks — section 194A, thresholds from 1 April 2025, Form 16A and Form 15G/15H
section: 194A / 393
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to:
topic: tds
verified: partial

Banks, co-operative banks and the post office deduct TDS on interest (other than savings-account interest) under section 194A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 at 10% when the interest paid to you in a financial year exceeds the threshold. The Finance Act, 2025 raised the threshold from 1 April 2025 to ₹50,000 for ordinary depositors (from ₹40,000) and to ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens (from ₹50,000). If you have not given your PAN, TDS is deducted at 20%. The bank issues Form 16A, a TDS certificate, and many banks also give an annual interest certificate showing interest credited and TDS per account. If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can submit Form 15G (or Form 15H if you are a senior citizen) to the bank so that no TDS is deducted. Interest below the threshold is paid without TDS but is still taxable and still appears in your AIS. From 1 April 2026 TDS on interest is governed by section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

## [IT-FORM16-RECONCILE] What Form 16 does not include, and what to check before filing
section: 203 / 192
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to:
topic: tds
verified: partial

Form 16 only covers salary paid by that employer and the tax deducted on it. It usually does not include your bank or FD interest, capital gains, rent or freelance income, unless you declared that income to your employer during the year; all such income must still be added in your return. Before filing, check that: (1) the gross salary in Form 16 Part B matches your salary slips and the salary shown in your AIS, since arrears or bonuses paid late can make them differ; (2) the TDS in Form 16 Part A (quarterly deposits) matches the tax deducted shown in Part B and in Form 26AS, because only TDS that appears in 26AS is credited to you; (3) the PAN on Form 16 and on interest certificates is your own and correct, otherwise the TDS credit goes to the wrong PAN; (4) deductions shown in Part B are within their limits, for example section 80C is capped at ₹1.5 lakh; and (5) interest in your bank certificates and AIS is included in the return. Under-reporting income that appears in AIS can lead to an intimation under section 143(1) or a notice.

## [IT-TWO-EMPLOYERS] Two employers in one year — combine salaries, standard deduction only once
section: 192 / 16
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to:
topic: salary
verified: partial

If you changed jobs during the financial year you receive a Form 16 from each employer. Each employer computes TDS only on the salary it paid, and usually gives the full standard deduction (₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old regime) and the lower slab rates again, so the total TDS is often less than the tax actually due on your combined salary. In your return you must add the salaries from both Form 16s, claim the standard deduction only once, and pay any shortfall as self-assessment tax (with interest under sections 234B/234C if applicable) before filing. To avoid this, give your previous employer's salary and TDS details to the new employer in Form 12B when you join. Two Form 16s alone do not stop you from using ITR-1.
