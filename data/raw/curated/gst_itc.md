## [GST-ITC-CONDITIONS] Basic conditions for claiming input tax credit
section: 16
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: itc
verified: yes

Input tax credit (ITC) lets a registered person reduce output GST by the GST paid on purchases used in business (section 16(1) of the CGST Act). Under section 16(2), ITC is allowed only if the buyer has a tax invoice or debit note from a registered supplier; the supplier has reported it in GSTR-1 and it appears in the buyer's GSTR-2B; the goods or services have actually been received; the supplier has paid the tax to the government; and the buyer has filed GSTR-3B. If the buyer does not pay the supplier within 180 days of the invoice date, the ITC must be reversed with interest, and can be reclaimed once paid. Under section 16(4), ITC for a financial year must be claimed by 30 November of the following year or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier.

## [GST-ITC-BLOCKED] Blocked credits under section 17(5) and no ITC for composition dealers
section: 17(5), 10(4)
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: itc_blocked
verified: yes

Section 17(5) of the CGST Act blocks ITC on certain items even if used in business. Examples: motor vehicles for carrying up to 13 persons, including the driver, unless the business supplies such vehicles, transports passengers or runs a driving school, plus their insurance and repairs; food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services and cosmetic surgery; life and health insurance; club and gym memberships; travel benefits for employees on leave; goods or services for construction of immovable property on own account; goods lost, stolen, destroyed or given as free samples or gifts; and tax paid under composition. So a freelancer cannot claim GST paid on a personal car or on restaurant meals. A composition dealer cannot claim any ITC at all (section 10(4)), and services taxed at 5% without ITC, such as salons and budget hotels, cannot use credit against that tax.
