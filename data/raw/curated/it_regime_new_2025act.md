## [IT-NEW-SLABS-TY2627] Section 202 — New tax regime slab rates for Tax Year 2026-27
section: 202
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: slabs
verified: yes

Section 202(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (which replaces section 115BAC of the 1961 Act) sets the default new-regime rates for individuals, HUFs, AOPs, BOIs and artificial juridical persons from Tax Year 2026-27: total income up to ₹4,00,000 – nil; ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 – 5%; ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 – 10%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 – 15%; ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 – 20%; ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 – 25%; above ₹24,00,000 – 30%. The rates are the same as for FY 2025-26; Budget 2026 did not change the slabs. Unlike the 1961 Act, the slabs are written into the Act itself. The same rates apply to senior and super-senior citizens. A 4% health and education cess is added on tax plus surcharge.

## [IT-NEW-REBATE-TY2627] Section 156 — rebate up to ₹60,000 if total income is up to ₹12 lakh (Tax Year 2026-27)
section: 156
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: rebate
source_url: https://wm.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-156-84
verified: yes

Section 156 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces section 87A of the 1961 Act. Under section 156(2), a resident individual taxed under the new regime of section 202(1) gets a rebate equal to 100% of tax or ₹60,000, whichever is less, if total income does not exceed ₹12,00,000. So there is effectively no tax up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income (₹12.75 lakh of salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction). For income above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief applies: the tax payable cannot exceed the amount by which total income exceeds ₹12,00,000. Section 156(1) keeps the old-regime rebate of up to ₹12,500 for total income up to ₹5,00,000. Only resident individuals qualify; HUFs, firms and non-residents do not.

## [IT-NEW-REBATE-EXPLAINED] Why "no tax up to ₹12 lakh" and "5% from ₹4 lakh" are both true (new regime, FY 2025-26 and Tax Year 2026-27)
section: 156
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to:
topic: rebate
law: general
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1
verified: yes
short_answer: Both are true, because tax is worked out in two steps. Step 1, the slabs: 5% on ₹4–8 lakh (₹20,000) and 10% on ₹8–12 lakh (₹40,000), so ₹60,000 of tax at ₹12 lakh of taxable income. Step 2, the rebate: section 156 (section 87A of the old Act) cancels up to ₹60,000 of tax for a resident individual whose total income is not more than ₹12 lakh, so the tax you actually pay is nil. For a salaried person the ₹75,000 standard deduction stretches this to about ₹12.75 lakh of salary. Above ₹12 lakh the rebate is lost and every slab is taxed (at ₹16 lakh: ₹1,20,000 plus 4% cess), with marginal relief easing the jump just above ₹12 lakh.

Many taxpayers are confused because the new-regime slab table charges 5% from ₹4 lakh, yet people say income up to ₹12 lakh is tax-free. Both are true. First, tax is worked out on the slabs: 5% on ₹4–8 lakh (₹20,000) and 10% on ₹8–12 lakh (₹40,000), so ₹60,000 at ₹12 lakh of taxable income. Then the rebate (section 87A of the 1961 Act for FY 2025-26, section 156 of the 2025 Act from Tax Year 2026-27) cancels tax of up to ₹60,000 for a resident individual whose total income is not more than ₹12 lakh, so the tax becomes nil. For a salaried person the ₹75,000 standard deduction makes this about ₹12.75 lakh of salary. Above ₹12 lakh the rebate is lost and every slab is taxed, not only the part above ₹12 lakh: at ₹16 lakh taxable income tax is ₹1,20,000 plus 4% cess. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps the tax at the income above ₹12 lakh.

## [IT-NEW-STDDED-TY2627] Section 19 — standard deduction ₹75,000 (new regime) or ₹50,000 (old regime) from Tax Year 2026-27
section: 19
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: standard_deduction
source_url: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-19-206
verified: yes

Section 19 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 lists the deductions from salary income and replaces section 16 of the 1961 Act. The standard deduction is ₹75,000 or the amount of salary, whichever is less, where income-tax is computed under the new regime of section 202(1), and ₹50,000 or the salary, whichever is less, in any other case (the old regime). The amounts are unchanged from FY 2025-26. It is available to salaried employees and pensioners automatically, with no proof needed. Self-employed people and freelancers with only business or professional income do not get it.

## [IT-NEW-DEDUCTIONS-TY2627] Section 202(2) — exemptions and deductions not allowed in the new regime (Tax Year 2026-27)
section: 202(2)
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: new_regime_deductions
verified: partial

Under section 202(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, total income in the new regime is computed without most exemptions in Schedule III (such as house rent allowance and leave travel concession) and without the deductions in Chapter VIII, except a few carved out: section 124(1) and 124(2) (pension scheme contributions, including the employer's NPS contribution, the old section 80CCD(2), up to 14% of salary), section 125(2) (Agniveer Corpus Fund) and section 146. So the old 80C (now section 123), 80D (now 126), 80TTA/80TTB (now 153), HRA and self-occupied home-loan interest cannot be claimed. Loss from house property cannot be set off against other heads. The ₹75,000 standard deduction under section 19 remains available.

## [IT-NEW-OPTOUT-TY2627] Section 202(4) — choosing the old regime from Tax Year 2026-27
section: 202(4)
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: regime_choice
verified: yes

The new regime of section 202(1) is the default under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Section 202(4) lets a person opt out. Someone without business or professional income (for example a salaried employee) exercises the option along with the return of income under section 263(1) for that tax year, so the choice can be made fresh every year. A person with business or professional income must exercise the option on or before the due date under section 263(1); once exercised it continues for later years and can be withdrawn only once, after which the person can never opt out again unless they stop having business income. The 1961 Act equivalent was section 115BAC(6) with Form 10-IEA; the corresponding form under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 has a new number.
