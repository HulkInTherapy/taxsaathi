## [GST-REG-THRESHOLD] GST registration threshold limits (goods, services, special category states)
section: 22
effective_from: 2019-04-01
effective_to:
topic: registration
verified: yes

Under section 22 of the CGST Act, a supplier must register for GST when aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds Rs 20 lakh (Rs 10 lakh in special category states). Using the power to raise this for suppliers dealing exclusively in goods, Notification 10/2019-Central Tax raised the limit for goods-only suppliers to Rs 40 lakh from 1 April 2019. The Rs 40 lakh option does not apply in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Puducherry, Sikkim, Telangana, Tripura and Uttarakhand, nor to sellers of ice cream, pan masala or tobacco. In summary: goods-only suppliers Rs 40 lakh in most states (Rs 20 lakh in the listed states, Rs 10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura); service providers, including freelancers, Rs 20 lakh (Rs 10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). The 56th GST Council FAQ (September 2025) confirmed these thresholds did not change under GST 2.0.

## [GST-REG-AGG-TURNOVER] What counts in "aggregate turnover" for the GST threshold
section: 2(6)
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: registration
verified: yes

Aggregate turnover under section 2(6) of the CGST Act is the total value of all taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports of goods or services and inter-state supplies made by persons having the same PAN, computed on an all-India basis. It excludes GST itself (CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess) and the value of inward supplies on which you pay tax under reverse charge. Section 22 adds that supplies made on behalf of principals are included. So a shopkeeper with two shops in different states under one PAN must add both shops' sales, and exempt sales such as fresh vegetables also count towards the limit, even though a person dealing only in exempt supplies need not register.

## [GST-REG-COMPULSORY] Compulsory GST registration irrespective of turnover
section: 24
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: registration
verified: yes

Section 24 of the CGST Act requires certain persons to register even if turnover is below the threshold. These include persons making inter-state taxable supplies; casual taxable persons; persons liable to pay tax under reverse charge; non-resident taxable persons; persons required to deduct TDS under section 51; agents supplying on behalf of other taxable persons; input service distributors; persons supplying goods or services through an e-commerce operator that collects TCS under section 52; every such e-commerce operator; and suppliers of online information and database access services or online money gaming from outside India to Indian consumers. Two relaxations exist: service providers making inter-state supplies are exempt up to the normal services threshold (Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax), and from 1 October 2023 small goods sellers on e-commerce platforms can sell within their own state without registration, subject to conditions.

## [GST-REG-FREELANCER] GST registration for freelancers, consultants and content creators
section: 22
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: registration_freelancer
verified: partial

Freelancers, consultants, designers, developers, tutors and content creators supply services, so the services threshold applies: registration is required once aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds Rs 20 lakh (Rs 10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). Billing clients in other states does not by itself force registration for service providers below this limit, because Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax exempts inter-state service suppliers up to the threshold. Most professional services, such as consulting, software, design and advertising, are taxed at 18%. Income from foreign clients is an export of services, which is zero-rated: once registered, the freelancer can export without paying IGST by filing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT, Form GST RFD-11), but export receipts still count in aggregate turnover. Detailed export and refund questions should be taken to a CA.

## [GST-REG-VOLUNTARY] Voluntary GST registration below the threshold
section: 25(3)
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: registration
verified: yes

Section 25(3) of the CGST Act lets a person who is not liable to register under section 22 or 24 take GST registration voluntarily. Once registered, all provisions of the Act apply as for any registered person: the business must charge GST on its taxable supplies, issue tax invoices, file returns such as GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B (or CMP-08 and GSTR-4 under composition), and can claim input tax credit on business purchases. Small businesses often register voluntarily because registered B2B buyers prefer suppliers whose invoices give them input tax credit, or because online marketplaces ask for a GSTIN. The downside is the compliance burden and late fees if returns are missed.

## [GST-REG-PROCESS] How to apply for GST registration (Form GST REG-01) and timelines
section: 25
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: registration_process
verified: yes

A person who becomes liable to register must apply within 30 days of becoming liable (section 25(1)); casual and non-resident taxable persons must apply at least 5 days before starting business. The application is filed online on the GST portal (gst.gov.in) in Form GST REG-01 using PAN, mobile number and email, with documents such as proof of place of business and bank details, and Aadhaar authentication of the promoter or authorised signatory (rule 8). Under rule 9 the officer must approve within 7 working days if the application is in order. If Aadhaar authentication is not done, or the system or officer flags the case for physical verification, registration is granted within 30 days after verification. If the officer needs clarification, a notice in REG-03 is issued and the applicant replies in REG-04 within 7 working days.

## [GST-REG-SIMPLIFIED] Simplified GST registration for small and low-risk businesses (Rule 14A) from 1 November 2025
section: Rule 14A
effective_from: 2025-11-01
effective_to:
topic: registration_process
verified: partial

The 56th GST Council recommended an optional simplified registration scheme, operational from 1 November 2025 (CGST Rule 14A, notified by Notification 18/2025-Central Tax). An applicant who, on self-assessment, expects output tax on supplies to registered persons (B2B) not to exceed Rs 2.5 lakh per month, including CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and cess, can opt for it in Form GST REG-01. Registration is granted automatically and electronically within three working days, subject to Aadhaar authentication. Only one registration per PAN per state is allowed under the scheme, and a person can later withdraw from it. The government said this would cover about 96% of new applicants.
