## [IT-FL-44ADA] Presumptive taxation for professionals — section 44ADA (FY 2024-25, FY 2025-26)
section: 44ADA
law: old_act
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: presumptive
verified: yes

Under section 44ADA of the Income-tax Act, 1961, a resident individual or partnership firm (not an LLP) in a specified profession — such as software or IT consulting treated as technical consultancy, engineering, architecture, medicine, law, accountancy, interior decoration or film artists — can declare 50% of gross receipts as taxable profit without keeping detailed books. The scheme is available if gross receipts do not exceed ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh where cash receipts are not more than 5% of total receipts. You can declare more than 50% but not less (unless you keep books and get an audit). No separate deduction for expenses is allowed beyond the 50% assumption. The return is filed in ITR-4 by 31 August 2026 for FY 2025-26.

## [IT-FL-44AD] Presumptive taxation for small businesses — section 44AD: 8% or 6% of turnover
section: 44AD
law: old_act
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: presumptive
verified: yes

Section 44AD of the Income-tax Act, 1961 lets a resident individual, HUF or firm (not an LLP) running an eligible business declare profit at 8% of turnover, or 6% of turnover received through banking channels or digital modes. It is available if turnover does not exceed ₹2 crore, or ₹3 crore where cash receipts are not more than 5% of turnover. It does not cover professionals under section 44AA (they use 44ADA), commission or brokerage income, or agency business. If a person opts out of 44AD after using it, they cannot come back for the next five years. Returns are filed in ITR-4; there is no requirement to maintain books of account.

## [IT-FL-SEC58-TY2627] Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 — presumptive taxation from Tax Year 2026-27
section: 58
law: new_act
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: presumptive
verified: partial

From Tax Year 2026-27, sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE of the 1961 Act are consolidated into a single section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, in a table format. The core rules carry over: eligible professionals declare 50% of gross receipts as profit (receipts up to ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are within 5%); eligible small businesses declare 8% of turnover, or 6% of digital/banking receipts (turnover up to ₹2 crore, or ₹3 crore if cash is within 5%); and goods-carriage owners with up to ten vehicles use per-vehicle income. Commentators note some tightening, such as stricter disqualification for minor commission income and a full bar on setting off losses against presumptive income. The tax-audit provision (old section 44AB) corresponds to section 63.

## [IT-FL-ADVANCE-TAX] Advance tax: ₹10,000 threshold and 15/45/75/100% instalments
section: 208 / 211 / 404
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: advance_tax
source_url: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-404-5
verified: partial

If your tax liability for the year, after subtracting TDS and TCS, is ₹10,000 or more, you must pay advance tax during the year itself — section 208 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, continued as section 404 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The cumulative instalments are: at least 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December and 100% by 15 March. A resident senior citizen (60+) with no business or professional income is exempt from advance tax. Salaried employees usually need not pay it because the employer deducts TDS, but a salaried person with large interest, capital gains or freelance income on the side may have to. Pay online through the e-filing portal using challan ITNS 280.

## [IT-FL-ADVANCE-PRESUMPTIVE] Advance tax for presumptive taxpayers and interest for late payment
section: 211 / 234B / 234C / 424
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: advance_tax
verified: partial

Freelancers and businesses using presumptive taxation (section 44AD or 44ADA, now section 58) need not pay four instalments: they can pay 100% of their advance tax in a single instalment on or before 15 March of the year. If advance tax is not paid or is short, interest is charged: under section 234B (section 424 of the Income-tax Act, 2025), simple interest of 1% per month if advance tax paid is less than 90% of the assessed tax; and under section 234C, 1% per month for deferment of individual instalments (for presumptive taxpayers, only for a shortfall at 15 March). Example: a freelancer with ₹30 lakh receipts under 44ADA declares ₹15 lakh profit and should pay the full advance tax by 15 March.

## [IT-FL-TDS-194J] TDS on freelance professional fees — section 194J (section 393)
section: 194J / 393
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to:
topic: tds
verified: partial

When a business or professional client pays a resident freelancer fees for professional services, it must deduct TDS under section 194J of the Income-tax Act, 1961 — 10% for professional fees and royalty, 2% for fees for technical services and certain other payments — once payments to that person cross ₹50,000 in a financial year (threshold raised from ₹30,000 by the Finance Act, 2025, from 1 April 2025). Contract work falls under section 194C instead (1% individuals, 2% others). From 1 April 2026 these TDS provisions (192 to 194T) are consolidated into sections 392 (salary) and 393 (other payments) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, with similar rates. If you do not give your PAN, TDS is deducted at a higher rate of 20%. TDS is only a prepayment; it is adjusted against your final tax.

## [IT-FL-26AS-AIS] Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS)
section: 285BB
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: tds
verified: partial

Form 26AS is your annual tax credit statement on the e-filing portal: it shows TDS and TCS deducted against your PAN, advance tax and self-assessment tax you paid, and refunds. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) is a wider report showing income and transactions reported by banks, employers, mutual funds and others — salary, interest, dividends, securities sales, rent and high-value transactions — with a simplified Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS). Before filing, freelancers should match their gross receipts and TDS with 26AS/AIS, because the department compares the return with this data and mismatches can lead to notices. You can give feedback in AIS if any entry is wrong.

## [IT-FL-FORM16] Form 16 — the TDS certificate for salaried employees
section: 203 / 392
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: tds
verified: partial

Form 16 is the certificate an employer issues for tax deducted from salary under section 192 of the Income-tax Act, 1961; it must be issued by 15 June after the end of the financial year. Part A shows TAN, PAN and quarterly TDS deposited; Part B shows the salary break-up, exemptions such as HRA, the standard deduction, Chapter VI-A deductions, the regime chosen and the tax computed. A freelancer paid professional fees instead receives Form 16A, a quarterly TDS certificate from each client. From 1 April 2026 salary TDS is governed by section 392 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and the certificate has a new number under the Income-tax Rules, 2026; the content remains similar. For FY 2025-26 salaries, Form 16 is issued under the 1961 Act.
