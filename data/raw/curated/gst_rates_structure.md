## [GST-STRUCT-PRE20] GST rate slabs before GST 2.0 (1 July 2017 to 21 September 2025)
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rate_structure
verified: yes

From the launch of GST on 1 July 2017 until 21 September 2025, goods and services were taxed under a four-tier structure of 5%, 12%, 18% and 28%, with nil-rated and exempt items below it. The 28% slab covered items such as air-conditioners, cement, cars and motorcycles, large TVs and aerated drinks. On top of GST, a separate GST compensation cess was charged on many 28% goods, for example cars, aerated drinks, pan masala and tobacco. The 12% slab covered items such as butter, ghee, cheese, namkeen and most hotel rooms up to Rs 7,500 a night, while the 18% slab covered most services and many FMCG goods like soap, shampoo and toothpaste. GST is levied under section 9 of the CGST Act at the rate notified by the government. Use this chunk for any question about a period up to 21 September 2025, for example "GST rate in August 2025" or "FY 2024-25".

## [GST-STRUCT-20] GST 2.0: new two-slab structure from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rate_structure
verified: yes

The 56th GST Council meeting, held in New Delhi on 3 September 2025 under Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman, approved the "next-generation GST reforms", popularly called GST 2.0. The old four-tier structure was rationalised into two main rates: a merit rate of 5% and a standard rate of 18%, plus a special de-merit rate of 40% for a small list of sin and luxury goods and services. The 12% and 28% slabs were largely removed: most 12% items moved to 5% and most 28% items moved to 18%. Some items became nil, such as UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer, Indian breads like roti and paratha, and individual life and health insurance. The new rates on services and on all goods except tobacco products and pan masala apply from 22 September 2025. The Council's FAQ confirms that the GST registration threshold did not change as part of this reform.

## [GST-CESS-STATUS] GST compensation cess: what happened after GST 2.0
section: GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: compensation_cess
verified: partial

GST compensation cess was an extra levy, over and above GST, on luxury and demerit goods such as cars, aerated drinks, tobacco and pan masala. Under GST 2.0, the Council decided to end the compensation cess levy and to fold its effect into the new 40% rate. From 22 September 2025, cars, motorcycles above 350cc and sugary or caffeinated drinks no longer carry compensation cess; they pay GST alone (18% or 40%). For pan masala, cigarettes, chewing tobacco, unmanufactured tobacco and bidi, the old GST rate plus cess continued until the cess loans were repaid. From 1 February 2026 the compensation cess on these tobacco goods was made nil, and they moved to the new GST rates. Pan masala now also attracts a separate Health Security se National Security (HSNS) Cess and tobacco products an additional central excise duty, both outside GST.

## [GST-RATE-TOBACCO-PRE] GST on pan masala, cigarettes and tobacco up to 31 January 2026
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2026-01-31
topic: rates_sin_goods
verified: yes

Pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco such as zarda, unmanufactured tobacco and bidi were kept outside the 22 September 2025 rate change. The 56th GST Council decided that these goods would continue at their existing rates of GST and compensation cess until the loans and interest under the compensation cess account were fully repaid. So for any period up to 31 January 2026, pan masala, cigarettes and tobacco products were taxed at 28% GST plus the applicable compensation cess, and bidi at 28%. A shop selling cigarettes or pan masala in, say, October 2025 therefore still charged 28% GST plus cess, even though most other goods had already moved to GST 2.0 rates.

## [GST-RATE-TOBACCO-POST] GST on pan masala, cigarettes and tobacco from 1 February 2026
section: 9
effective_from: 2026-02-01
effective_to:
topic: rates_sin_goods
verified: partial

From 1 February 2026 the deferred GST 2.0 rates for tobacco goods came into force. Pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, chewing tobacco and other manufactured tobacco and unmanufactured tobacco are taxed at the 40% de-merit rate, while bidi is taxed at 18%. GST on these goods is computed on the declared retail sale price (RSP) instead of transaction value. The GST compensation cess on these goods was reduced to nil from the same date. In place of the cess, pan masala manufacturers pay a capacity-based Health Security se National Security (HSNS) Cess, and cigarettes and other tobacco products pay an additional central excise duty; both are outside GST and are charged over and above the 40% GST.
