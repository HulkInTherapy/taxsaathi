## [IT-DED-80C] Section 80C (now section 123): up to ₹1.5 lakh for PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, tuition fees, home-loan principal
section: 80C / 123
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
source_url: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-123-96
verified: yes

Section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 allows an individual or HUF to deduct up to ₹1,50,000 a year for specified savings and payments: employee provident fund (EPF) contribution, Public Provident Fund (PPF), ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premium, National Savings Certificate, 5-year tax-saving fixed deposit, Sukanya Samriddhi, children's tuition fees and principal repayment of a home loan. The ₹1.5 lakh cap is shared with 80CCC and 80CCD(1). From Tax Year 2026-27 the same deduction continues as section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, with the eligible items listed in Schedule XV and the same ₹1,50,000 limit. It is available only under the old regime; the new regime (section 115BAC / section 202) does not allow it.

## [IT-DED-80CCD1B] Extra ₹50,000 for your own NPS contribution — section 80CCD(1B) (section 124)
section: 80CCD(1B) / 124
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: yes

Over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit of section 80C, section 80CCD(1B) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 allows an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 for your own contribution to the National Pension System (NPS). This means a person in the old regime can claim up to ₹2,00,000 in total through 80C plus NPS. The provision continues within section 124 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (pension scheme of the Central Government) from Tax Year 2026-27. It is an old-regime-only benefit: under the new regime your own NPS contribution is not deductible, though the employer's contribution is.

## [IT-DED-80CCD2] Employer's NPS contribution — section 80CCD(2), allowed in the new regime too
section: 80CCD(2) / 124(2)
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: yes

If your employer contributes to your NPS account, that amount is first added to your salary and then deducted under section 80CCD(2) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 124(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from Tax Year 2026-27). It is one of the few deductions allowed under the new regime. Limits: under the new regime, up to 14% of salary (basic plus DA) for all employers, private or government; under the old regime, 14% for Central or State Government employers and 10% for others. This deduction is outside the ₹1.5 lakh cap of section 80C. Asking the employer to restructure part of CTC as NPS is a common way to save tax in the new regime.

## [IT-DED-80D] Health insurance premium — section 80D (section 126): ₹25,000 / ₹50,000
section: 80D / 126
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: yes

Section 80D of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 126 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) gives a deduction for medical insurance premium. For yourself, spouse and dependent children the limit is ₹25,000 a year, or ₹50,000 if any of them is a senior citizen. A separate limit of ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if they are senior citizens) applies to premium for parents. Preventive health check-ups up to ₹5,000 are included within these limits, and premium must be paid by a mode other than cash (check-ups can be paid in cash). So a young employee paying for self and senior-citizen parents can claim up to ₹75,000. It is allowed only under the old regime.

## [IT-DED-80TTA-80TTB] Interest on savings and deposits — sections 80TTA and 80TTB (section 153)
section: 80TTA / 80TTB / 153
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: yes

Section 80TTA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 lets an individual below 60 deduct up to ₹10,000 of interest from savings bank accounts (not fixed deposits). Resident senior citizens instead use section 80TTB, which allows up to ₹50,000 of interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits and post-office deposits; a senior who claims 80TTB cannot also claim 80TTA. In the Income-tax Act, 2025 both are merged into section 153 (interest on deposits) from Tax Year 2026-27, with the same limits. Both are available only under the old regime. Interest itself is taxable as "income from other sources" and must be reported.

## [IT-DED-24B] Home-loan interest — section 24(b) (section 22): up to ₹2 lakh for a self-occupied house
section: 24(b) / 22
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: yes

Under section 24(b) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 22 of the Income-tax Act, 2025), interest on a loan taken to buy or build a self-occupied house is deductible up to ₹2,00,000 a year (₹30,000 if the loan is for repairs or taken before 1 April 1999), with construction to be completed within five years. This is allowed only under the old regime. For a let-out property, interest is deductible without limit in both regimes, but under the old regime the house-property loss that can be set off against salary is capped at ₹2,00,000, with the balance carried forward for 8 years; under the new regime the loss cannot be set off at all. Principal repayment falls under section 80C, not 24(b).

## [IT-DED-HRA-1961] HRA exemption under section 10(13A) — FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26
section: 10(13A)
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: hra
source_url: https://wm.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/allowances-allowable-to-tax-payer
verified: yes

A salaried employee who lives in rented accommodation can exempt part of house rent allowance under section 10(13A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 and Rule 2A. The exempt amount is the least of: (a) actual HRA received; (b) 50% of salary if the house is in Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi or Chennai, or 40% elsewhere; (c) rent paid minus 10% of salary. "Salary" here means basic plus dearness allowance (if part of retirement benefits) plus turnover-based commission. Example: basic ₹6 lakh, HRA ₹2.4 lakh, rent ₹2.4 lakh in Pune → least of 2.4L, 2.4L (40%) and 1.8L = ₹1,80,000 exempt. If annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh you must give the landlord's PAN. HRA exemption is available only under the old regime; for FY 2025-26 returns, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad are still non-metro (40%).

## [IT-DED-HRA-TY2627] HRA exemption from Tax Year 2026-27 — eight metro cities at 50%
section: Schedule III / Income-tax Rules 2026
law: new_act
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: hra
verified: partial

From Tax Year 2026-27, the HRA exemption continues under the Income-tax Act, 2025 as an exemption listed in Schedule III (the corresponding provision of section 10(13A) of the 1961 Act), with the calculation rules in the Income-tax Rules, 2026. The formula is unchanged — least of actual HRA, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% (metro) or 40% (non-metro) of salary — but the Rules 2026 reportedly expand the 50% metro list from four cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai) to eight, adding Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. Employees must also disclose their relationship with the landlord in the employer declaration form. HRA remains unavailable under the new regime of section 202.

## [IT-DED-80E] Education-loan interest — section 80E (section 129)
section: 80E / 129
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: partial

Section 80E of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 129 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from Tax Year 2026-27) allows an individual to deduct the entire interest paid on a loan taken from a bank or approved institution for higher education of self, spouse, children or a student for whom the individual is legal guardian. There is no upper monetary limit, but only interest (not principal) qualifies, and the deduction is available for the year repayment of interest starts plus the next seven years — eight years in total. It is useful for first-jobbers repaying a student loan, but it is allowed only under the old regime.

## [IT-DED-80G] Donations — section 80G (section 133)
section: 80G / 133
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: deductions
verified: partial

Section 80G of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (section 133 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from Tax Year 2026-27) gives a deduction for donations to approved funds and charitable institutions. Depending on the recipient, the deduction is 100% or 50% of the donation, and for some institutions it is limited to 10% of adjusted gross total income. Donations to funds such as the PM National Relief Fund qualify at 100% without limit. Cash donations above ₹2,000 are not deductible, and the donee's details from Form 10BE should match the return. The deduction is available only under the old regime.
