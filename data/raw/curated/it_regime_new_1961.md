## [IT-NEW-SLABS-FY2425] New tax regime slabs under section 115BAC for FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26)
section: 115BAC
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2025-03-31
topic: slabs
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-3
verified: yes

For FY 2024-25 (assessment year 2025-26), the default new tax regime under section 115BAC(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 had these slabs for individuals of every age: income up to ₹3,00,000 – nil; ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 – 5%; ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 – 10%; ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 – 15%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000 – 20%; above ₹15,00,000 – 30%. In cumulative terms, tax is ₹20,000 at ₹7 lakh, ₹50,000 at ₹10 lakh, ₹80,000 at ₹12 lakh and ₹1,40,000 at ₹15 lakh. Salaried taxpayers got a standard deduction of ₹75,000 under this regime for FY 2024-25. Health and education cess of 4% is added. These slabs were replaced from FY 2025-26 by the Finance Act, 2025.

## [IT-NEW-REBATE-FY2425] Section 87A rebate under the new regime for FY 2024-25: ₹25,000 up to ₹7 lakh
section: 87A
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2025-03-31
topic: rebate
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-3
verified: yes

For FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26), a resident individual taxed under the new regime of section 115BAC(1A) could claim a rebate under section 87A of up to ₹25,000 if total income did not exceed ₹7,00,000. Since slab tax on exactly ₹7 lakh is ₹20,000, the rebate in practice wiped out the whole tax up to ₹7 lakh of taxable income (about ₹7.75 lakh salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction). Marginal relief applied just above ₹7 lakh: the tax payable could not exceed the amount by which total income exceeded ₹7,00,000. Example: taxable income ₹7,15,000 gives slab tax ₹21,500, but tax is limited to ₹15,000. Non-residents cannot claim the rebate. Some e-filing pages show "₹20,000" for this year, which reflects the tax at ₹7 lakh, not the statutory cap.

## [IT-NEW-SLABS-FY2526] New tax regime slabs under section 115BAC for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)
section: 115BAC
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: slabs
verified: yes

For FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), the Finance Act, 2025 revised the new-regime slabs in section 115BAC(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961: income up to ₹4,00,000 – nil; ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 – 5%; ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 – 10%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 – 15%; ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 – 20%; ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 – 25%; above ₹24,00,000 – 30%. The same slabs apply to all ages, including senior citizens. Cumulative tax is ₹20,000 at ₹8 lakh, ₹60,000 at ₹12 lakh, ₹1,20,000 at ₹16 lakh, ₹2,00,000 at ₹20 lakh and ₹3,00,000 at ₹24 lakh, before 4% cess. The standard deduction for salaried employees stays at ₹75,000. This is the regime that applies to the return filed in 2026.

## [IT-NEW-REBATE-FY2526] Section 87A rebate up to ₹60,000 and marginal relief for FY 2025-26
section: 87A
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: rebate
verified: yes

For FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), a resident individual under the new regime of section 115BAC(1A) gets a rebate under section 87A of up to ₹60,000 if total income does not exceed ₹12,00,000. Because slab tax at ₹12 lakh is exactly ₹60,000, there is no income tax up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income, which for a salaried person means about ₹12.75 lakh of salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Marginal relief applies just above ₹12 lakh: tax cannot exceed the income above ₹12,00,000. Example: taxable income ₹12,25,000 gives slab tax ₹63,750 but tax is limited to ₹25,000 (₹26,000 with 4% cess). The rebate does not apply to income taxed at special rates, such as capital gains under sections 111A and 112A. Under the old regime the rebate is only ₹12,500 up to ₹5 lakh.

## [IT-NEW-STDDED-1961] Standard deduction under section 16(ia): ₹75,000 new regime, ₹50,000 old regime (FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26)
section: 16(ia)
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: standard_deduction
source_url: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/deductions/allowances-allowed-to-a-salaried-employee
verified: yes

Under section 16(ia) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, every salaried employee and pensioner gets a flat standard deduction from salary income without any bills or proof. For FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26, it is ₹75,000 or the amount of salary, whichever is lower, if you are taxed under the new regime of section 115BAC(1A), and ₹50,000 or the salary, whichever is lower, under the old regime. Freelancers and consultants who earn professional fees, not salary, do not get the standard deduction. The same amounts continue under section 19 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from Tax Year 2026-27.

## [IT-NEW-DEDUCTIONS-1961] Deductions allowed and not allowed under the new regime (section 115BAC)
section: 115BAC
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: new_regime_deductions
verified: yes

Under the new regime of section 115BAC of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26), most exemptions and Chapter VI-A deductions are not allowed. You cannot claim section 80C (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF), 80CCD(1B) self NPS, 80D health insurance, 80E, 80G, 80TTA/80TTB, the HRA exemption under section 10(13A), LTA, or interest on a self-occupied home loan under section 24(b). What is still allowed: the ₹75,000 standard deduction, the employer's contribution to NPS under section 80CCD(2) up to 14% of salary for all employers, the Agniveer Corpus Fund deduction under section 80CCH, and interest on a let-out house property (but its loss cannot be set off against other income). The trade-off is the lower slab rates and the bigger rebate.

## [IT-NEW-DEFAULT-OPTOUT-1961] New regime is the default; how to opt for the old regime (section 115BAC(6))
section: 115BAC(6)
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: regime_choice
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/ITR1-FAQ
verified: yes

Since AY 2024-25 the new regime under section 115BAC is the default for individuals. A salaried person or anyone without business or professional income can choose the old regime afresh every year, simply by selecting it in the return (ITR-1 or ITR-2) filed by the due date under section 139(1); no separate form is needed. The employer may deduct TDS based on the regime you declare during the year, but the final choice is made in the return. A person with business or professional income (ITR-3/ITR-4) who wants the old regime must file Form 10-IEA on or before the section 139(1) due date. Such a person can switch back to the new regime only once in a lifetime.
