## [IT-OLD-SLABS-BELOW60] Old tax regime slabs for individuals below 60 years
section: First Schedule
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: slabs
verified: yes

Under the old (optional) tax regime, an individual below 60 years pays: income up to ₹2,50,000 – nil; ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 – 5%; ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 – 20%; above ₹10,00,000 – 30%. Cumulative tax is ₹12,500 at ₹5 lakh and ₹1,12,500 at ₹10 lakh, plus 4% cess. These rates have not changed for many years: they applied for FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 under the Income-tax Act, 1961, and the Finance Act, 2026 keeps the same basic exemption of ₹2,50,000 for Tax Year 2026-27 for people who opt out of section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The old regime allows deductions such as 80C, 80D, HRA and home-loan interest, and a standard deduction of ₹50,000.

## [IT-OLD-SLABS-SENIOR] Old regime slabs for senior (60–80) and super-senior (80+) citizens
section: First Schedule
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: slabs
verified: yes

The old regime gives older residents a higher exemption limit. A resident senior citizen aged 60 to below 80 pays nil up to ₹3,00,000, 5% on ₹3,00,001 to ₹5,00,000, 20% on ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 and 30% above ₹10,00,000 (tax ₹10,000 at ₹5 lakh). A resident super-senior citizen aged 80 or more pays nil up to ₹5,00,000, 20% on ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 and 30% above ₹10,00,000. These limits applied in FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26 and continue for Tax Year 2026-27 under the Finance Act, 2026. The new regime has no age-based slabs: its nil band of ₹4 lakh (₹3 lakh in FY 2024-25) is the same for everyone.

## [IT-OLD-REBATE] Old regime rebate: up to ₹12,500 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh
section: 87A / 156(1)
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: rebate
verified: yes

A resident individual who chooses the old regime gets a rebate of up to ₹12,500 if total income (after deductions) does not exceed ₹5,00,000 — section 87A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26, and section 156(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from Tax Year 2026-27. Since old-regime tax at ₹5 lakh is exactly ₹12,500 (₹10,000 for seniors), tax becomes zero up to ₹5 lakh taxable income. There is no marginal relief in the old regime: at ₹5,00,001 the full slab tax of about ₹12,500 is payable. Compare the new regime: ₹60,000 up to ₹12 lakh from FY 2025-26, or ₹25,000 up to ₹7 lakh in FY 2024-25.

## [IT-OLD-DEDUCTIONS] What the old regime allows that the new regime does not
section: Chapter VI-A / Chapter VIII
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: old_regime_deductions
verified: yes

The old regime has higher slab rates but lets you reduce taxable income with exemptions and deductions. The main ones for salaried people are: standard deduction ₹50,000; HRA exemption; leave travel allowance; professional tax; section 80C investments up to ₹1,50,000 (section 123 of the 2025 Act); extra NPS contribution under 80CCD(1B) up to ₹50,000; health insurance under 80D (₹25,000, or ₹50,000 for seniors, plus parents); savings interest under 80TTA (₹10,000) or 80TTB for seniors (₹50,000); education-loan interest under 80E; donations under 80G; and up to ₹2,00,000 interest on a self-occupied home loan. As a rule of thumb, the old regime wins only if your total deductions are large — for most salaried people earning up to about ₹12.75 lakh, the new regime gives zero tax from FY 2025-26.
