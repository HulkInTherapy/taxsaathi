## [GST-INV-FIELDS] Mandatory details on a GST tax invoice
section: 31, Rule 46
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: invoicing
verified: yes

A registered person making a taxable supply must issue a tax invoice under section 31 of the CGST Act. Rule 46 lists what it must contain: name, address and GSTIN of the supplier; a consecutive serial number of up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year; date of issue; name, address and GSTIN of the recipient if registered; for unregistered buyers, name, delivery address and state when the value is Rs 50,000 or more; HSN code of goods or SAC of services; description; quantity and unit; total and taxable value after discount; GST rate and amount split into CGST, SGST/UTGST, IGST and cess; place of supply with state name for inter-state supplies; whether tax is payable on reverse charge; and the signature or digital signature of the supplier. E-invoices must also carry a QR code with the IRN.

## [GST-INV-BILLOFSUPPLY] Bill of supply instead of a tax invoice
section: 31(3)(c), Rule 49
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: invoicing
verified: partial

A registered person who supplies exempt goods or services, or who has opted for the composition scheme, cannot charge GST and must issue a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice (section 31(3)(c) and rule 49). A bill of supply contains the supplier's name, address and GSTIN, a consecutive serial number unique for the financial year, date of issue, recipient details if registered, HSN or SAC code, description, value of the supply after discount, and the supplier's signature. It shows no tax amount. A composition dealer must also write "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" on top of the bill. A small kirana under composition therefore gives customers a bill of supply, not a GST invoice.

## [GST-INV-HSN] How many HSN or SAC digits must be shown on invoices
section: Rule 46 proviso
effective_from: 2021-04-01
effective_to:
topic: invoicing_hsn
verified: yes

From 1 April 2021, Notification 78/2020-Central Tax fixed the number of HSN (goods) or SAC (services) digits on tax invoices by aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year. Taxpayers with turnover up to Rs 5 crore must show at least a 4-digit code, and those above Rs 5 crore must show 6 digits. A taxpayer with turnover up to Rs 5 crore may omit the HSN code on invoices issued to unregistered (B2C) customers, but must show 4 digits on B2B invoices. HSN-wise summaries are also reported in Table 12 of GSTR-1.

## [GST-INV-EINVOICE] E-invoicing threshold: aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore
section: Rule 48(4)
effective_from: 2023-08-01
effective_to:
topic: invoicing_einvoice
verified: yes

E-invoicing means reporting each B2B invoice to the government's Invoice Registration Portal (IRP), which returns an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a signed QR code that must be printed on the invoice (rule 48(4)). From 1 August 2023, Notification 10/2023-Central Tax made e-invoicing mandatory for registered persons whose aggregate turnover in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeded Rs 5 crore (the limit had been stepped down from Rs 500 crore in 2020 to Rs 10 crore by October 2022). It applies to B2B supplies, exports and credit and debit notes, not to sales to ordinary consumers. A business above the threshold that issues a B2B invoice without an IRN has not issued a valid invoice, and the buyer may lose input tax credit. A small shop below Rs 5 crore does not need e-invoicing.
