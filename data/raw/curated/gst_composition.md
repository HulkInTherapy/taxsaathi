## [GST-COMP-ELIGIBILITY] Who can opt for the GST composition scheme
section: 10
effective_from: 2019-04-01
effective_to:
topic: composition
verified: yes

The composition scheme under section 10 of the CGST Act is a simple, low-rate option for small businesses. A registered person whose aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year did not exceed Rs 1.5 crore can opt for it (Notification 14/2019-Central Tax, from 1 April 2019); the limit is Rs 75 lakh in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura and Uttarakhand. It is meant for traders, manufacturers and restaurants. A person cannot opt if he supplies goods or services not leviable to GST, makes inter-state outward supplies, is a casual or non-resident taxable person, or manufactures notified goods such as ice cream, pan masala and tobacco. If several registrations share one PAN, all must opt together. The option lapses on the day turnover crosses the limit during the year.

## [GST-COMP-RATES] Composition scheme tax rates for traders, manufacturers and restaurants
section: 10, Rule 7
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: composition
verified: yes

Rule 7 of the CGST Rules prescribes composition rates as a percentage of turnover in the state; each rate is charged once as CGST and once as SGST, so the total is double the rule's figure. Manufacturers (other than of notified goods) pay 0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST = 1% of turnover. Restaurants and other suppliers of food or drink served as a service (paragraph 6(b) of Schedule II) pay 2.5% + 2.5% = 5%. Traders and other eligible suppliers pay 0.5% + 0.5% = 1% of the turnover of taxable supplies, so exempt sales like fresh vegetables are excluded. These rates were not changed by GST 2.0 in September 2025. Composition dealers pay this tax from their own pocket; they cannot collect it from customers.

## [GST-COMP-RESTRICTIONS] Restrictions on composition dealers: no ITC, no tax collection, bill of supply
section: 10(4), 31(3)(c)
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: composition
verified: partial

A composition dealer cannot collect any tax from customers and cannot claim input tax credit on purchases (section 10(4)). Instead of a tax invoice, he issues a bill of supply, which must mention "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"; he must also display this on his signboard. He cannot make inter-state outward supplies, so a composition shopkeeper in Pune cannot sell to a buyer in Gujarat. He can sell goods within his own state through an e-commerce operator that collects TCS (allowed from 1 October 2023 under Notification 36/2023-CT), but cannot supply services through e-commerce operators. A goods trader or manufacturer under composition may also supply services (other than restaurant services) up to 10% of the previous year's state turnover or Rs 5 lakh, whichever is higher.

## [GST-COMP-SERVICES] Composition scheme for service providers (6% scheme under section 10(2A))
section: 10(2A)
effective_from: 2019-04-01
effective_to:
topic: composition_services
verified: yes

Service providers and mixed suppliers who are not eligible for the regular composition scheme can opt for a separate scheme under section 10(2A) of the CGST Act (first introduced by Notification 2/2019-Central Tax (Rate) from 1 April 2019). The condition is that aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year did not exceed Rs 50 lakh. Tax is paid at 3% CGST + 3% SGST, i.e. 6% of the turnover of supplies in the state (rule 7). As with regular composition, the person cannot make inter-state outward supplies, cannot supply services through e-commerce operators that collect TCS, cannot make non-taxable supplies, cannot collect tax from customers, and cannot claim input tax credit. A local freelancer or tutor with only in-state clients and turnover under Rs 50 lakh may consider this scheme.

## [GST-COMP-FORMS] Composition scheme forms and due dates (CMP-02, CMP-08, GSTR-4)
section: Rule 62
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to:
topic: composition_returns
verified: yes

To opt into composition for a financial year, an existing registered person files intimation in Form GST CMP-02 on the GST portal before the start of that year; a new applicant can choose composition in the registration application. A composition taxpayer then has two filings under rule 62: Form GST CMP-08, a quarterly statement-cum-challan for paying self-assessed tax, due by the 18th of the month after each quarter (for example, 18 October for July to September); and Form GSTR-4, the annual return. From FY 2024-25 onwards, GSTR-4 is due by 30 June following the end of the financial year (earlier it was 30 April). Tax in CMP-08 must be paid in cash from the electronic cash ledger. Composition dealers do not file the monthly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns that regular taxpayers file.
