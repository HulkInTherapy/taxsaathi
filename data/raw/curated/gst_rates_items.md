## [GST-RATE-AC-PRE] GST on ACs, TVs, dishwashers, fridges and washing machines before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_consumer_durables
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, air-conditioners (HSN 8415) and dishwashing machines (HSN 8422) were taxed at 28% GST. Television sets and monitors larger than 32 inches were also at 28%, while TVs and monitors up to 32 inches were at 18%. Refrigerators and washing machines were not in the 28% slab during this period: they had already been cut from 28% to 18% from 27 July 2018. So an AC bought in August 2025 carried 28% GST, a 43-inch LED TV 28%, and a refrigerator or washing machine 18%. These are the old, pre-GST 2.0 rates and apply to sales up to 21 September 2025.

## [GST-RATE-AC-POST] GST on ACs, TVs, dishwashers, fridges and washing machines from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_consumer_durables
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025 under GST 2.0, air-conditioning machines and dishwashing machines moved from 28% to 18% GST. All television sets, including LCD and LED TVs, and monitors and projectors are now uniformly taxed at 18%, whatever the screen size; earlier only TVs up to 32 inches were at 18%. Refrigerators and washing machines remain at 18%; GST 2.0 did not change their rate because they were already at 18%. So today an AC, a 55-inch TV, a dishwasher, a fridge or a washing machine all attract 18% GST.

## [GST-RATE-VEHICLE-PRE] GST on small cars, motorcycles and three-wheelers before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_vehicles
verified: partial

Up to 21 September 2025, all motor cars, motorcycles (of any engine size) and three-wheelers were taxed at 28% GST. Cars and bigger motorcycles also attracted compensation cess on top of GST, which varied with engine size, length and fuel type, so the total tax on a mid-size or large car or SUV was well above 28%. Buses, goods trucks, ambulances and many auto parts were also at 28%. These rates apply to vehicles bought up to 21 September 2025, for example a motorcycle bought in July 2025.

## [GST-RATE-VEHICLE-POST] GST on small cars, motorcycles and three-wheelers from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_vehicles
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, small cars moved from 28% to 18% GST. A small car means a petrol, LPG or CNG car with engine capacity up to 1200cc and length up to 4000 mm, or a diesel car up to 1500cc and 4000 mm. Motorcycles, scooters and mopeds up to 350cc, three-wheelers, buses, trucks and ambulances also moved from 28% to 18%, and all auto parts are uniformly at 18%. Mid-size and large cars (above these limits), SUVs above 1500cc with length over 4000 mm and ground clearance of 170 mm or more, and motorcycles above 350cc are taxed at 40%, with no compensation cess on top.

## [GST-RATE-CEMENT-PRE] GST on cement before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_construction
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, Portland cement, aluminous cement, slag cement and similar hydraulic cements (HSN 2523), including clinkers, were taxed at 28% GST, the highest regular slab. A builder, contractor or individual buying cement bags for house construction during this period paid 28% GST on the bill. A hardware or building-material shop selling cement had to charge 28% on such sales made up to 21 September 2025.

## [GST-RATE-CEMENT-POST] GST on cement from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_construction
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, the GST Council reduced GST on cement (HSN 2523: Portland, aluminous, slag, super-sulphate and similar hydraulic cements, including clinkers) from 28% to 18%. This was one of the headline changes of GST 2.0 meant to reduce construction and housing costs. A building-material dealer selling cement today must charge 18% GST, and a registered buyer using it in business can claim input tax credit at 18%, subject to the normal ITC rules.

## [GST-RATE-PERSONALCARE-PRE] GST on hair oil, soap, shampoo and toothpaste before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_fmcg
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, everyday personal-care products were mostly taxed at 18% GST. This covered hair oil and shampoo (HSN 3305), toothpaste and dental floss (HSN 3306), toilet soap bars (HSN 3401), talcum and face powder (HSN 3304), and shaving cream and aftershave (HSN 3307). Tooth powder was taxed at 12%. A kirana or general store selling a bottle of hair oil or a tube of toothpaste in this period charged 18% GST.

## [GST-RATE-PERSONALCARE-POST] GST on hair oil, soap, shampoo and toothpaste from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_fmcg
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, GST on common personal-care items was cut to 5%. Hair oil, shampoo, toothpaste, dental floss, toilet soap bars, talcum and face powder and shaving cream and aftershave moved from 18% to 5%, tooth powder moved from 12% to 5%, and toothbrushes are also at 5%. Bicycles, tableware, kitchenware and many household articles also moved to 5%. So toothpaste, soap and shampoo are no longer at 18%; a shop selling them today should charge 5% GST.

## [GST-RATE-FOOD-PRE] GST on butter, ghee, cheese and packaged food before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_food
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, butter, ghee, butter oil and dairy spreads (HSN 0405), cheese (HSN 0406), condensed milk and pre-packaged namkeen and bhujia were taxed at 12% GST. Chocolates, biscuits, pastries and cakes, cornflakes, ice cream, sauces, instant noodles, coffee extracts and many other packaged foods were at 18%. UHT milk and pre-packaged, labelled paneer were at 5%, and paratha and similar Indian breads at 18%. These old rates apply to sales made up to 21 September 2025.

## [GST-RATE-FOOD-POST] GST on butter, ghee, cheese and packaged food from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_food
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, butter, ghee, cheese, condensed milk and pre-packaged namkeen and bhujia moved from 12% to 5% GST. Chocolates, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, cornflakes, pasta, instant noodles, sauces, soups and coffee moved from 18% to 5%. UHT milk and pre-packaged, labelled paneer (chena) became nil-rated (from 5%), and all Indian breads such as roti, chapati, paratha and parotta are now nil. A dairy or sweet shop selling packaged ghee or cheese today charges 5% GST.

## [GST-RATE-INSURANCE-PRE] GST on life and health insurance premiums before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_insurance
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, individual life insurance and individual health insurance premiums were taxed at 18% GST as financial services (SAC heading 9971), with input tax credit available to the insurer. A freelancer paying a Rs 20,000 health insurance premium for a family floater policy in this period would have paid about Rs 3,600 as GST on top, i.e. 18%.

## [GST-RATE-INSURANCE-POST] GST on life and health insurance premiums from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_insurance
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, GST on all individual life insurance policies, whether term, ULIP or endowment, and on all individual health insurance policies, including family floater and senior-citizen policies, was exempted (18% to nil). Reinsurance of these policies is also exempt. So an individual buying a term plan or a personal health policy pays no GST on the premium today. The exemption is for individual policies; group policies taken by employers are not covered by this change.

## [GST-RATE-SIN-PRE] GST on aerated drinks, luxury cars and gaming before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_sin_goods
verified: partial

Up to 21 September 2025, aerated waters and other drinks containing added sugar or flavouring, caffeinated beverages and carbonated fruit drinks were taxed at 28% GST plus compensation cess. Mid-size and large cars, SUVs and motorcycles above 350cc were taxed at 28% GST plus compensation cess. Betting, casinos, gambling, horse racing, lottery and online money gaming were taxed at 28%, as was admission to casinos, race clubs and sporting events like the IPL. Personal aircraft and yachts were also at 28% plus cess.

## [GST-RATE-SIN-POST] The 40% de-merit rate from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_sin_goods
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, a special 40% GST rate applies to a short list of sin and luxury items, with no compensation cess on top. It covers aerated waters and all drinks with added sugar or flavouring, caffeinated beverages and carbonated fruit drinks (28% to 40%); mid-size and large cars and SUVs, motorcycles above 350cc, personal aircraft and yachts (28% to 40%); and betting, casinos, gambling, horse racing, lottery and online money gaming, plus admission to casinos, race clubs and sporting events like the IPL (28% to 40%). Pan masala and tobacco moved to 40% only from 1 February 2026. Plain unsweetened mineral water moved from 18% to 5%.

## [GST-RATE-APPAREL-PRE] GST on clothes and footwear before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_apparel
verified: partial

Before GST 2.0, the price cut-off for clothes and footwear was Rs 1,000. Readymade garments and clothing accessories with a sale value up to Rs 1,000 per piece were taxed at 5%, and those above Rs 1,000 at 12%. Footwear up to Rs 1,000 per pair was taxed at 12% (raised from 5% with effect from 1 January 2022), and footwear above Rs 1,000 per pair at 18%. A cloth shop selling a Rs 1,800 kurta in August 2025 therefore charged 12% GST.

## [GST-RATE-APPAREL-POST] GST on clothes and footwear from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_apparel
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, the cut-off for clothes and footwear is Rs 2,500. Apparel, clothing accessories and made-up textile articles with a sale value up to Rs 2,500 per piece are taxed at 5%; above Rs 2,500 per piece they are taxed at 18% (up from 12%). Footwear with a sale value up to Rs 2,500 per pair is taxed at 5% (down from 12%), and footwear above Rs 2,500 per pair stays at 18%. So a Rs 1,800 kurta is at 5% today, but a Rs 3,000 jacket is at 18%.

## [GST-RATE-SERVICES-PRE] GST on hotel rooms, salons and gyms before 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2017-07-01
effective_to: 2025-09-21
topic: rates_services
verified: yes

Up to 21 September 2025, hotel accommodation with a value up to Rs 7,500 per unit per day was taxed at 12% GST with input tax credit; rooms above Rs 7,500 were at 18%. Beauty and physical well-being services, including salons, barbers, beauty parlours, gyms, health clubs, fitness centres and yoga centres, were taxed at 18% with input tax credit. A salon owner billing a customer in August 2025 therefore charged 18% GST.

## [GST-RATE-SERVICES-POST] GST on hotel rooms, salons and gyms from 22 September 2025
section: 9
effective_from: 2025-09-22
effective_to:
topic: rates_services
verified: yes

From 22 September 2025, hotel accommodation valued up to Rs 7,500 per unit per day is taxed at 5% without input tax credit (earlier 12%); rooms above Rs 7,500 stay at 18%. Beauty and physical well-being services, including salons, barbers, gyms, health clubs, fitness centres and yoga centres, are taxed at 5% without input tax credit (earlier 18%). "Without ITC" means the salon, gym or hotel cannot claim credit of GST paid on its own purchases against this 5% output tax. Movie tickets up to Rs 100 moved from 12% to 5%.
