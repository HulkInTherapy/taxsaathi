## [IT-ITR1] ITR-1 (Sahaj) eligibility for AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26)
section: 139
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: itr_forms
verified: yes

ITR-1 (Sahaj) is the simplest return, for a resident (ordinarily resident) individual with total income up to ₹50 lakh from salary or pension, up to two house properties (raised from one property for AY 2026-27), other sources such as interest, agricultural income up to ₹5,000, and long-term capital gains under section 112A (listed shares/equity funds) up to ₹1.25 lakh. You cannot use ITR-1 if you have business or professional income, any short-term capital gains, LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh, are a company director, held unlisted equity shares, have foreign assets or foreign income, or have losses to carry forward. A typical first-job salaried employee with salary, a savings account and an FD files ITR-1. Due date: 31 July 2026.

## [IT-ITR2] ITR-2 for individuals with capital gains or other complex non-business income (AY 2026-27)
section: 139
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: itr_forms
verified: partial

ITR-2 is for individuals and HUFs who do not have income from business or profession but cannot use ITR-1 — for example, total income above ₹50 lakh, short-term capital gains or LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh from shares or mutual funds, sale of property, more than two house properties, foreign assets or income, being a company director, holding unlisted shares, or losses to carry forward. Non-residents and RNORs with salary also use ITR-2. Its due date for AY 2026-27 is 31 July 2026, the same as ITR-1. A salaried person who sold mutual funds at a short-term gain during FY 2025-26 must therefore switch from ITR-1 to ITR-2.

## [IT-ITR3] ITR-3 for freelancers and professionals with regular books (AY 2026-27)
section: 139
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: itr_forms
verified: partial

ITR-3 is for individuals and HUFs having income from business or profession that is not declared on a presumptive basis, or who otherwise cannot use ITR-4 — for example, a freelancer who keeps books and declares actual profit, who has capital gains along with business income, is a partner in a firm, trades in futures and options, or has income above ₹50 lakh. It covers salary, house property, capital gains and other income as well. For AY 2026-27 the due date is 31 August 2026 if no tax audit is required, and the audit-case date (31 October 2026, extended by CBDT to 21 November 2026) if accounts must be audited under section 44AB.

## [IT-ITR4] ITR-4 (Sugam) for presumptive income under 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE (AY 2026-27)
section: 44AD / 44ADA / 44AE
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: itr_forms
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/itr%204-faqs
verified: yes

ITR-4 (Sugam) is for a resident individual, HUF or firm (other than an LLP) with total income up to ₹50 lakh that includes business or professional income computed on a presumptive basis under section 44AD (business), 44ADA (specified professionals such as software consultants, doctors, designers) or 44AE (up to ten goods carriages). It can also include salary, up to two house properties, interest, agricultural income up to ₹5,000 and LTCG under section 112A up to ₹1.25 lakh. It cannot be used with short-term capital gains, income above ₹50 lakh, or as a company director. The due date for AY 2026-27 is 31 August 2026. A freelancer wanting the old regime must file Form 10-IEA before that date.

## [IT-ITR-FORMS-TY2627] ITR forms for Tax Year 2026-27 under the Income-tax Act, 2025
section: 263
law: new_act
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: itr_forms
verified: partial

Returns for Tax Year 2026-27, the first year under the Income-tax Act, 2025, will be filed in 2027 under section 263 using return forms prescribed under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. As of October 2026 the new return forms for Tax Year 2026-27 are expected to be notified before 1 April 2027. The Income-tax Rules, 2026 renumbered almost all forms, so the return forms may carry new names or numbers instead of the familiar ITR-1 to ITR-7; check the final form names and eligibility limits on the e-filing portal once notified. Until then, the AY 2026-27 rules — ₹50 lakh income cap for ITR-1/ITR-4, LTCG under section 112A (now the corresponding capital-gains section of the 2025 Act) up to ₹1.25 lakh — are the best guide.
