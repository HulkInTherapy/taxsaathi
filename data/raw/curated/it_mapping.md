## [IT-MAP-80C] Section 80C (1961) = Section 123 (2025 Act); 80CCC/80CCD = Section 124
section: 80C -> 123
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
source_url: https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new
verified: partial

Section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 corresponds to Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Yes, the 80C deduction still exists from Tax Year 2026-27, only under a new number: section 123 allows up to ₹1,50,000 for the payments listed in Schedule XV (PF, PPF, life insurance, ELSS, tuition fees, home-loan principal and so on). The pension deductions of sections 80CCC and 80CCD of the 1961 Act — including 80CCD(1B) self NPS and 80CCD(2) employer NPS — correspond to section 124 of the 2025 Act. Chapter VI-A of the 1961 Act (sections 80A to 80U) as a whole becomes Chapter VIII of the 2025 Act, roughly sections 123 to 154. For FY 2025-26 returns filed in 2026, you still quote section 80C.

## [IT-MAP-80D-80E-80G] Section 80D = 126, 80E = 129, 80G = 133, 80GG = 134
section: 80D -> 126
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
source_url: https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new
verified: partial

Health-insurance deduction: Section 80D of the Income-tax Act, 1961 corresponds to Section 126 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Education-loan interest: Section 80E of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 129 of the 2025 Act. Donations: Section 80G of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 133 of the 2025 Act. Rent paid by people who get no HRA: Section 80GG of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 134 of the 2025 Act. Person with disability: Section 80U corresponds to Section 154. The amounts and conditions are carried over; what changes from Tax Year 2026-27 is the section number you cite. All of these are available only if you opt out of the new regime of section 202.

## [IT-MAP-80TTA-80TTB] Sections 80TTA and 80TTB (1961) = Section 153 (2025 Act)
section: 80TTA/80TTB -> 153
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
source_url: https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025-section-numbers-old-vs-new
verified: partial

Section 80TTA (savings-account interest up to ₹10,000) and Section 80TTB (deposit interest up to ₹50,000 for resident senior citizens) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 are merged into a single Section 153 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, titled deduction in respect of interest on deposits. The limits are unchanged from Tax Year 2026-27. As before, a senior citizen gets the higher ₹50,000 limit covering fixed deposits too, and others get ₹10,000 on savings interest only. Section 153 is not available in the default new regime of section 202.

## [IT-MAP-87A-115BAC] Section 87A (1961) = Section 156; Section 115BAC = Section 202
section: 87A -> 156; 115BAC -> 202
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/objective-and-scope-new-act
verified: yes

The rebate of section 87A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 corresponds to Section 156 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (₹12,500 up to ₹5 lakh in section 156(1); ₹60,000 up to ₹12 lakh for the new regime in section 156(2), with marginal relief). The new tax regime of section 115BAC of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025; the slabs are now written into section 202(1) itself. Relief under section 89 for salary arrears corresponds to section 157 of the 2025 Act. So "87A rebate" in FY 2025-26 language means the "section 156 rebate" from Tax Year 2026-27.

## [IT-MAP-SALARY-HP] Section 16 = Section 19; Section 24(b) = Section 22; HRA 10(13A) = Schedule III
section: 16 -> 19; 24 -> 22; 10(13A) -> Schedule III
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
verified: partial

Deductions from salary in Section 16 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (including the standard deduction in 16(ia)) correspond to Section 19 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Deductions from house-property income in Section 24 of the 1961 Act, including the ₹2 lakh home-loan interest limit of 24(b), correspond to Section 22 of the 2025 Act. The exemptions of section 10 of the 1961 Act were moved into Schedules of the 2025 Act; the HRA exemption of section 10(13A) corresponds to an entry in Schedule III (exempt allowances), with the formula in the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Exact Schedule serial numbers were not confirmed from an official mapping table.

## [IT-MAP-PRESUMPTIVE-AUDIT] Sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE = Section 58; 44AB tax audit = Section 63
section: 44AD/44ADA/44AE -> 58; 44AB -> 63
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/objective-and-scope-new-act
verified: yes

The three presumptive-taxation provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961 — Section 44AD (small business, 8%/6% of turnover), Section 44ADA (professionals, 50% of receipts) and Section 44AE (goods carriages) — are consolidated into a single Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which sets them out in one table. Tax audit under Section 44AB of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 63 of the 2025 Act (this pairing is from secondary sources). A freelancer who used 44ADA for FY 2025-26 will use section 58 for Tax Year 2026-27.

## [IT-MAP-RETURN-FEES] Section 139 = Section 263 (return of income); 234F = Section 428
section: 139 -> 263; 234F -> 428
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
verified: partial

Filing of the return of income under Section 139 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 corresponds to Section 263 of the Income-tax Act, 2025: 139(1) original return due dates → 263(1), 139(4) belated return → 263(4), 139(5) revised return → 263(5). The late-filing fee of Section 234F of the 1961 Act corresponds to Section 428 of the 2025 Act, which also absorbs the fee for late revised returns (old section 234I). Section 427 corresponds to the fee for late TDS/TCS statements (old section 234E). The Finance Act, 2026 substituted sections 427 and 428 to consolidate these fees.

## [IT-MAP-TDS] TDS sections 192 to 194T = Sections 392 and 393
section: 192 -> 392; 194C/194J -> 393
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
verified: partial

All TDS provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (sections 192 to 194T) are consolidated in the Income-tax Act, 2025: TDS on salary under Section 192 corresponds to Section 392, and TDS on other payments — contractors (194C), professional and technical fees (194J), interest (194A), rent (194I), and so on — corresponds to Section 393, which lists them in tables. Section 197A (Form 15G/15H declarations) corresponds to section 393(6). The higher TDS rate when the payee has no PAN (old section 206AA) has a corresponding provision in the 2025 Act; secondary sources cite section 397, but this could not be confirmed officially. Payments made from 1 April 2026 follow the new sections.

## [IT-MAP-ASSESSMENT] Section 143 = Section 270; reassessment 147–153 = Sections 279–286
section: 143 -> 270; 147-153 -> 279-286
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
verified: partial

Assessment provisions are renumbered in the Income-tax Act, 2025. Processing and scrutiny assessment under Section 143 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 corresponds to Section 270 of the 2025 Act. The income-escaping-assessment provisions, sections 147 to 153 of the 1961 Act (including the notice under section 148), correspond to sections 279 to 286 of the 2025 Act, with section 148 reported as section 280. Notices for FY 2025-26 and earlier years can still be issued under the 1961 Act because of the savings clause in section 536 of the 2025 Act.

## [IT-MAP-ADVANCE-TAX] Advance tax: Section 208 = 404, 211 = 408; interest 234B = 424; previous year = tax year (s.3)
section: 208 -> 404; 211 -> 408; 234B -> 424
effective_from: 2024-04-01
effective_to:
topic: mapping
verified: partial

The obligation to pay advance tax when liability is ₹10,000 or more, Section 208 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, corresponds to Section 404 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The instalment schedule of Section 211 (15 June, 15 September, 15 December, 15 March) corresponds to Section 408 of the 2025 Act, and the exemption for resident senior citizens without business income (old section 207) continues. Interest for default in advance tax under Section 234B corresponds to Section 424. Finally, the "previous year" of section 3 and the "assessment year" of section 2(9) of the 1961 Act are replaced by the single "tax year" defined in Section 3 of the 2025 Act.
