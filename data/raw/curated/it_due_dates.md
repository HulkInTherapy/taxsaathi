## [IT-DUE-FY2526] ITR due dates for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), filed in 2026
section: 139(1)
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: due_dates
verified: yes

For income of FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), returns are filed in 2026 under section 139(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Due dates: 31 July 2026 for salaried individuals, pensioners and others without business income (ITR-1 and ITR-2); 31 August 2026 for business or professional taxpayers whose accounts need no audit, including freelancers filing ITR-3 or ITR-4 under 44ADA — a new date announced in Budget 2026 (earlier 31 July); 31 October 2026 for tax-audit cases, which CBDT extended to 21 November 2026 (with the audit report date moved from 30 September to 21 October 2026); and 30 November 2026 for transfer-pricing cases. Filing on time is needed to choose the old regime if you have business income and to carry forward most losses.

## [IT-BELATED-REVISED-FY2526] Belated return, revised return and late fees for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)
section: 139(4) / 139(5) / 234F / 234I
effective_from: 2025-04-01
effective_to: 2026-03-31
topic: due_dates
source_url: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/ITR1-FAQ
verified: yes

If you miss the due date for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), you can file a belated return under section 139(4) up to 31 December 2026, paying a late fee under section 234F of ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh) plus interest under section 234A on unpaid tax. To correct a mistake you can file a revised return under section 139(5). From AY 2026-27 the revised-return window is extended to 31 March 2027 (12 months from the end of the financial year, instead of 31 December). A revision filed after 31 December 2026 attracts a fee under section 234I: ₹1,000 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh, ₹5,000 otherwise. An updated return (ITR-U) can be filed later, up to 48 months from the end of the assessment year, with additional tax.

## [IT-DUE-TY2627] Return due dates for Tax Year 2026-27 under section 263 (filed in 2027)
section: 263(1)

effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: due_dates
source_url: https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/income-tax-return-filing-dates-and-revised-itr-fees-for-tax-year-2026-27-when-you-have-to-pay-5000/article-188803
verified: partial

For Tax Year 2026-27, the first year under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the return of income under section 263(1) is due on: 31 July 2027 for salaried individuals and others without business income (the ITR-1/ITR-2 type cases); 31 August 2027 for persons with business or professional income whose accounts are not required to be audited, such as most freelancers on presumptive taxation, and partners of such firms; 31 October 2027 for companies and audit cases; and 30 November 2027 for transfer-pricing cases. The 31 August date for non-audit business cases was introduced by the Finance Act, 2026 (Budget 2026-27). CBDT can extend these dates by notification. A business taxpayer who wants the old regime must exercise the section 202(4) option by the section 263(1) due date.

## [IT-BELATED-REVISED-TY2627] Belated and revised returns and late fee (section 428) for Tax Year 2026-27
section: 263(4) / 263(5) / 428
law: new_act
effective_from: 2026-04-01
effective_to:
topic: due_dates
verified: partial

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, a belated return for Tax Year 2026-27 under section 263(4) can be filed within nine months from the end of the tax year (by 31 December 2027) or before assessment is completed, whichever is earlier. A revised return under section 263(5) can be filed within twelve months from the end of the tax year (by 31 March 2028), as extended by the Finance Act, 2026. Section 428 replaces section 234F of the 1961 Act: the fee for a late return is ₹1,000 if total income does not exceed ₹5 lakh and ₹5,000 otherwise, and section 428(b) charges the same ₹1,000/₹5,000 for a revised return filed after nine months from the end of the tax year. Interest on unpaid tax is extra.
